## Results

**P1 free key art.** Both calls came back as convincing terrain-only maps in the
concept's register: palette, darkness, rock and roof rendering are close. Open
grass lum 91 / 94 (concept 97), hue 57 (concept 62, so slightly warmer/yellower),
S 0.55-0.57 (match). Forest canopy lum 51-54 (concept 56). Attaching the full
concept (P1a) leaked no units, no UI, no grid lines; at most the city's street
plan has a few hex-angled streets. P1a came back 1672x941, P1b 1536x1024.
Differences from the concept: Codex is crisper and more illustrative (the
concept is soft), grass has more visible fine strokes, trees and houses read
as slightly oblique 3/4 miniatures, scattered single trees dot the grass, and
in P1a elements are about 1.3-1.5x larger relative to the map.

**P2 guided key art.** Registration is excellent. P2a: forest-mass centroids
1.7-10 px off their hex centres (0.007-0.039 of hex width), river band median
offset 4 px (p90 9.5, max 12.5; 0.016 hex width), band width matches the guide
(30 px). 88% of forest pixels and 90% of settlement pixels land in the right
hexes. But P2a painted forests as hard cookie-cutter hexagons, and the city as
hexagons with hex-shaped lanes. P2b asked for organic outlines: forests became
natural ragged clumps and still sat 3-10 px off centre (spill 5-13% into
neighbours), river offset median 2 px (water band a bit narrower, 25 px). The
city stays hex-ish. P2b adds lots of scattered bushes on open grass. Hills land
in the right hexes but ridges spill a little across edges (73-79% contained).

**P3 ground swatch.** Both are on-palette but too smooth and slightly
oversaturated. P3a calm: lum 98.4, H 62, S 0.66, V 0.42, lum std 4.0, which
reads as flat felt. P3b mottled: lum 91.1, H 63, S 0.67, V 0.39, lum std 6.7;
its mottling is blobby dabs, not the concept's fine streaky grass. Hue and
value land well; saturation is high because blue is too low (33-37 vs 50).

**P4 feature sheets on magenta.** All three came back as 9 clean, separated
subjects on uniform magenta (about (250,2,250), std ~1.3). Forests: perfect
hexagon silhouettes (368x321, ratio 1.15), nine near-identical, canopy hue 92
(bluer and darker than concept, lum 47). Hills: each on its own grass patch
(brighter than concept grass), diamond-ish footprint, oblique view. Settlements:
5 towns + 4 cities as asked, clearly oblique 3/4 view with visible facades,
footprints about 1.5:1 wide, so not top-down.

**P5 in-context paint-in.** The strongest result. Codex kept the magenta
(99.6-99.7%) and the hexagon silhouettes (IoU 0.993-0.996, 0-1 px shift) and
painted features inside with a grass rim. The rim ground is re-rendered, not
copied: mean abs RGB diff 7.5-9 and 3.5-5% of rim pixels differ by more than
30. It is about 1-4 lum darker, with a dark contact shadow round the forest. P5a
forests: dense, natural, crowns a little big and blobby. P5b: hills, villages and
city blocks all good and in register, but buildings are chunkier than the
concept (house about 1/8 of hex width vs about 1/12), and the view is oblique.

## Takeaways

- Route by class: grass ground from a swatch, but it needs a stronger texture
  prompt or post-processing. Forest, hills, town and city via P5 paint-in on our
  own ground hexes (keeps silhouette and rim; composite only the interior over
  the original ground to hide the re-rendered rim). Rivers and roads via
  guided key art (P2), which tracks a drawn path to a few px; cut tiles from it
  or use it as the edge-connection reference.
- Wording that helped: "fills ONLY its own hexagon ... grass showing around it"
  (precise placement); "Keep EXACTLY as they are: ..." (paint-in preservation);
  describing the concept palette as dark yellow-olive (#666632) instead of the
  sites set's cool-olive wording.
- Wording that hurt or did nothing: "shaped to fill a flat-top hexagon" gives
  literal hex cut-outs; "straight top-down" did not stop the oblique 3/4 view on
  buildings and hills; "organic outline" fixed forests but not the city.
- Surprising: the full concept attachment did not leak units or UI at all, and
  the guide's flat code colours did not leak into the paint.

### Base: four growth stages as one compound

Footprint (assumption, to be confirmed): nested rhombus in `hex.ts` axial offsets from
the anchor — stage 1 `(0,0)`; 2 `+(1,0)` (SE, edge 0); 3 `+(0,1)` (S, edge 5); 4
`+(1,1)`. Each stage is painted as one composition by
`scripts/tile-foundry/paintin_cluster.py`: the union of the stage's hexes (340 px per

(distance to the union outline), internal edges are kept exactly as painted.

internal edges match the neighbour, fails loudly on a moved cluster).
